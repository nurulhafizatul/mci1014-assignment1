# 01 Problem Statement

## Problem description
As a postgraduate student living on a fixed monthly allowance/stipend, I repeatedly run short of money 5-8 days before month end. Spending is spread across e-wallets (Touch 'n Go, GrabPay), bank transfers, cash and food delivery apps. I do not record it consistently, so I cannot see overspending until it is too late. Evidence: in the last month, small daily purchases (food, delivery, subscriptions) were not tracked and the balance reached near zero on day 23.

## Problem owner
The student (myself), who owns the monthly budget and makes all spending decisions.

## Affected users
- Student account holder (primary).
- Family member or sponsor who sends extra money in emergencies (secondary).
- Roommates when shared bills (rent, utilities) are paid late.

## Decision to support
Each day: "Can I afford this purchase today without breaking my monthly budget?" Each week: "Should I reduce spending in a category?"

## Observable value
- Fewer days with a balance below RM 20 before month end (target: 0 days, from ~7 days).
- Weekly review takes under 5 minutes.
- At least 90% of transactions categorised correctly after the first month.

## Possible harm
- Wrong categorisation leads to a misleading budget and bad decisions.
- Financial data exposed (privacy, security).
- Over-reliance: student stops thinking about spending and trusts a wrong AI forecast.
- Anxiety or shame from overly strict alerts.

## Non-AI alternative
A manual spreadsheet or notebook with fixed category envelopes (e.g. Food RM 400, Transport RM 100) and a weekly 10-minute review, using bank/e-wallet statements. It is cheap, private and fully transparent, but needs discipline and takes time to categorise each entry. The AI-enabled application is only justified if it reduces categorisation effort while keeping accuracy and privacy at least as good as this baseline.
